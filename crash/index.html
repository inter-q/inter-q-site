CRASH.EXE — collision speed calculator
What CRASH does

Enter what was measured at the scene. Each calculator gives a result (a minimum speed, a drag factor or a stopping distance) and shows every step of the math, so you can follow it line by line.

Checking a hand calculation? Enter the same numbers you used, then type your answer in Check your work under the result. CRASH tells you whether it matches and, if it doesn't, the most likely slip.

CASE.EXECase number, date and time, location, investigator, vehicle and road. SKID.EXESpeed from a skid to a stop. Use one length or each tire mark, with braking efficiency and grade. COMBINED.EXESkid across more than one surface, or skid plus a known impact speed. DRAG.EXEFind the drag factor from test skids, a drag sled or an accelerometer. YAW.EXECritical speed from a curved yaw mark (chord and middle ordinate). STOP.EXEStopping distance from a speed: reaction plus braking. PRINT.EXEPrint the worksheet with all the work shown. Save the case to a file, or open a saved one. HELPFormulas, where the 30 comes from, a drag factor table and the limits of these estimates.

SKID.EXE — skid to stop
Skid length

Measure each tire mark from where it starts to where that tire stopped. If front and rear tires marked the same line, subtract the wheelbase.

Leave a wheel blank if it left no mark.

Each wheel counts its share of the weight only for the distance it was locked. This assumes all the marks end where the car stopped. Most front-engine cars carry 55–65% of their weight on the front axle.

Drag and braking
Grade

Uphill means the car was going uphill as it skidded. Grade % = rise ÷ run × 100.

Check your work

COMBINED.EXE — more than one surface
S = √(S₁² + S₂² + S₃² …)

Work out each stretch on its own, then combine the squares. Energy adds; speeds don't. Add a known speed (for example, impact speed from a momentum analysis) as its own line. Uphill means the car was going uphill on that stretch.

YAW.EXE — critical speed from a yaw mark

Measure a chord across the yaw mark, then the middle ordinate: the distance from the chord's midpoint to the mark, at a right angle to the chord. Run the chord between two points on the outer edge of the mark. Use the first part of the mark, before the car rotates far.
